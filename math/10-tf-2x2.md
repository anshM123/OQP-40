# Conjecture TF holds for all 2 × 2 pairs

Ansh Mishra, Aryan Senthilkumar. 2026-10-11.

**Status.**
- **Main Theorem: proved, with a computer-assisted part.** Conjecture TF of
  [`08-general-lower-half-route.md`](08-general-lower-half-route.md) holds at every level, for every pair of positive
  definite 2 × 2 matrices A, B and every θ ∈ (0, 1).
- **The proof has four parts.**
  - **Theorem 1 (exact reduction): proved.** For a 2 × 2 pair, TF at every level is equivalent to one closed-form
    inequality. The new ingredient is a geometric lemma about the Weyl distribution of a 2 × 2 pair, the *lens
    lemma*. In particular TF holds whenever

    **(C)**  (Tr(AB^r) − λ₂^{N₀})^θ · (Tr(A⁻¹ #_{1−θ} B⁻¹) − λ₂⁻¹) ≥ 1,  λ₂ := λ_max(e^{Z_θ}).

  - **Proposition 2: proved.** (C) holds when the Golden–Thompson gap Tr(AB^r) − Tr e^{log A + r log B} is at least
    (det A)^{1/2}(det B)^{r/2}.
  - **Theorem 3 (bounded condition numbers): computer-assisted.** (C) holds when κ(A) ≤ e^{80} and
    κ(B)^{(1−θ)/θ} ≤ e^{80}, where κ is the condition number. The proof is analytic lemmas plus rigorous ball
    arithmetic (Arb) on 183,421 boxes, with a mechanical check that the boxes cover the region.
  - **Theorem 4 (the far region): proved.** (C) holds when κ(A) ≥ e^{80} or κ(B)^{(1−θ)/θ} ≥ e^{80}. The proof is
    elementary: explicit estimates from a Stieltjes representation of the weighted geometric mean.
- **Corollary 5.** TF holds, in every dimension, for pairs in which A and B each have at most two distinct
  eigenvalues.
- **Checks.**
  - We re-ran, from the published scripts, the smaller certification run, the ball-arithmetic verification of the
    constants of Theorem 4, and one seed of the check of its intermediate inequalities. All logs are identical.
  - We sampled the certified boxes with independent 80-digit code.
  - We checked every hypothesis of Theorem 4 at random far-region points with independent 150-digit code.
  - No discrepancy was found.
- **What this does and does not give.**
  - The lower half itself is classical for d = 2: Furuichi, Kuriyama and Yanagi (2009) prove it even for single
    words ([`03-lower-half.md`](03-lower-half.md), Proposition 2.4).
  - The point here is Conjecture TF, the conjectured route to the general lower half. Notes 08–09 had TF only in
    perturbative regimes. This is its first non-perturbative case, and the first class of pairs on which it is
    proved in full.
  - TF implies the lower half (note 08, Proposition 3) and says more: it controls the whole tilted tail, not only its
    total.
  - The lens lemma is false for d ≥ 3 (Section 6). So the general conjecture needs a different argument.
- Nothing here has been refereed externally.

**Notation** is that of notes 08 and 09:
- θ ∈ (0, 1/2] (the case θ > 1/2 follows by exchanging A and B), N₀ = 1/θ, r = (1−θ)/θ;
- ℓ(a, b) = θ log a + (1−θ) log b, and Z = θ log A + (1−θ) log B with eigenvalues ζ₁ ≤ ζ₂;
- Π = Π_{A,B} is the Weyl distribution, and by note 09, (0.1),

  T(u) = ⟨Π, g_u∘ℓ⟩ − g_u(ζ₁) − g_u(ζ₂),  g_u(v) = 1[v > u]·[e^{N₀v} − (N₀+1)e^{N₀u} + N₀e^{(N₀+1)u−v}] / (N₀(N₀+1)).

TF says T(u) ≥ 0 for every u ∈ R.

## 0. Setting

- **Commuting pairs.** Π = δ_{P₁} + δ_{P₂} at the joint eigenvalue points, and ℓ(P_i) = ζ_i. So T ≡ 0.
- **From now on A and B do not commute.** Write A = a₀ + a⃗·σ⃗ and B = b₀ + b⃗·σ⃗. Then a⃗ and b⃗ are linearly
  independent.
  - Let M be the 2 × 2 matrix with rows a⃗ᵀ, b⃗ᵀ, restricted to span(a⃗, b⃗); it is invertible.
  - Let D be the closed unit disk of that plane, Q₀ = (a₀, b₀), and w(y) = (1 − |y|²)^{−1/2}.
  - The joint numerical range is the ellipse W = Q₀ + MD. Put ℓ̃(y) = ℓ(Q₀ + My), v_min = min_D ℓ̃ and v_max = max_D ℓ̃.
- **Facts about ℓ̃.**
  - ℓ̃ is real-analytic and strictly concave near D. Its gradient θa⃗/(a₀ + a⃗·y) + (1−θ)b⃗/(b₀ + b⃗·y) is a positive
    combination of two independent vectors, so it never vanishes. Hence v_min and v_max are attained only on ∂D.
- **Ordering.** ζ₁ ≤ v_min ≤ (ζ₁ + ζ₂)/2 ≤ ζ₂ ≤ v_max.
  - *Outer inequalities (Jensen).* For a unit vector e, ⟨Ze, e⟩ ≤ ℓ(⟨Ae,e⟩, ⟨Be,e⟩), by concavity of log. Take e at a
    minimiser of ℓ on W, and then e a top eigenvector of Z.
  - *Middle inequality.* Let P_v = (⟨Av,v⟩, ⟨Bv,v⟩) for the two unit eigenvectors v of A⁻¹B, and z_v = ℓ(P_v). By note 09,
    Tool 0.3, Σ_v e^{−z_v} = G := Tr(A⁻¹ #_{1−θ} B⁻¹). This matrix has determinant e^{−Tr Z}, so AM–GM gives
    G ≥ 2e^{−Tr Z/2}. Hence min_v z_v ≤ Tr Z/2. Since the points P_v lie in W, v_min ≤ (ζ₁ + ζ₂)/2.

## 1. Theorem 1: reduction to one closed-form inequality

**1.1 The disk formula.** Note 09, Lemma 1 (the Bloch-sphere formula), together with the fact that the uniform
measure on S² projects to the density (2π)⁻¹w(y) on D, gives for every C¹ function F near W

  ⟨Π, F⟩ = (1/π) ∫_D ((1 + y·∇_y) F̃)(y) w(y) dy,  F̃(y) := F(Q₀ + My).   (1.1)

The right side makes sense for every Lipschitz F, because w ∈ L¹(D). We use it as the definition of ⟨Π, F⟩ for such F.

**1.2 The derivative of the tail.**
- **Formula.** ∂_u g_u(v) = −e^{N₀u} k(u − v), with k(w) = (1 − e^w)₊. Differentiating (1.1) under the integral sign gives

  T′(u) = −e^{N₀u} f(u),  f := m − κ,  m(u) := ⟨Π, k(u − ℓ)⟩,  κ(u) := Σ_i k(u − ζ_i).   (1.2)

- **Meaning.** m is the continuous density of μ^θ = Π^θ * k, so m ≥ 0 by Heinävaara's positivity theorem. κ is the
  density of the commuting reference κ^θ.

**1.3 The lens lemma.**
- **Definitions.** For u ∈ R put D_u = {y ∈ D : ℓ̃(y) > u}, Γ_u = {y ∈ D : ℓ̃(y) = u}, and

  Λ(u) := (1/π) [ ∫_{D_u} w dy + ∫_{Γ_u} (y·n̂) w dσ ],  n̂ = ∇ℓ̃/|∇ℓ̃|.

  This is ⟨Π, 1[ℓ > u]⟩, the mass that the projection Π^θ puts above u.
- **Lemma 1.1.** The functions e^{−u}m(u) and e^{−u}f(u) are Lipschitz. For almost every u,
  (e^{−u}m)′ = −e^{−u}Λ(u) and (e^{−u}f)′ = −e^{−u}(Λ(u) − #{i : ζ_i > u}).
  - *Proof.* On D_u, (1 + y·∇)k(u − ℓ̃) = 1 − e^{u−ℓ̃} + e^{u−ℓ̃}(y·∇ℓ̃), and it vanishes off D_u. The first two terms
    give −e^{−u}(1/π)∫_{D_u} w after multiplying by e^{−u} and differentiating. The last term is handled by the coarea
    formula (∇ℓ̃ ≠ 0) and gives the boundary term. Finally e^{−u}κ(u) = Σ_i (e^{−u} − e^{−ζ_i})₊. ∎
- **Lemma 1.2 (lens lemma).** For every u ∈ (v_min, v_max) except the finitely many critical values of ℓ̃ on ∂D,
  Λ(u) ≤ 1. More precisely,

  Λ(u) = 2 − k − (1/π) Σ_{j=1}^{k} ∫_{L_j} (1 − |y|²)^{−3/2} dy,

  where k ≥ 1 is the number of arcs of ∂D on which ℓ̃ ≤ u, and the L_j are the "lenses" defined in the proof.
- *Proof.*
  - **Additivity.** For a region R ⊂ D with piecewise smooth boundary meeting ∂D transversally, put
    Λ_R := (1/π)[∫_R w dy − ∫_{∂R∩D} (y·ν_R) w dσ], with ν_R the outer normal. Λ_R is finitely additive, because
    shared boundary pieces carry opposite normals. Λ(u) = Λ_{D_u}.
  - **(i) The disk.** Λ_D = (1/π)∫_D w = 2.
  - **(ii) Caps.** Let S = {y ∈ D : y·e > t} with |t| < 1. The push-forward of w dy to the e-axis has density π on
    [−1, 1], so ∫_S w = π(1 − t). On the chord y·ν_S = −t and ∫_chord w dσ = π. Hence Λ_S = 1 for every cap.
  - **(iii) Lenses.** Let L ⊂ D be a region whose closure meets ∂D only at finitely many corner points. Apply the
    divergence theorem to the field y·w(y), for which ∇·(yw) = (2 − |y|²)(1 − |y|²)^{−3/2}, on L ∩ {|y| ≤ 1 − ε} and let
    ε → 0. This gives Λ_L = −(1/π)∫_L (1 − |y|²)^{−3/2} dy ≤ 0.
  - **Decomposition.** Since v_min < u < v_max are attained on ∂D, the set {ℓ̃ ≤ u} ∩ ∂D is a union of k ≥ 1 closed
    arcs B_j, crossed transversally by Γ_u. Let c_j be the chord joining the endpoints of B_j, and S_j the cap it cuts
    off on the side of B_j; the caps are disjoint. The superlevel set K = {ℓ̃ > u} is convex, so K ∩ D contains
    D ∖ ∪_j S_j. Inside S_j the convex set K ∩ S_j contains c_j and avoids B_j, so it is a lens L_j bounded by c_j and
    an arc of Γ_u. Thus D_u = (D ∖ ∪_j S_j) ∪ ∪_j L_j, and (i)–(iii) give Λ(u) = (2 − k) + Σ_j Λ_{L_j} ≤ 1. ∎
- **Remarks.**
  - For a linear ℓ the lenses are empty and Λ ≡ 1, the rank of a spectral projection.
  - Λ(u) = 2 for u < v_min, so by the lemma Π^θ has an atom of mass at least 1 at v_min. This is why e^{−u}f turns
    from decreasing to nondecreasing there.

**1.4 Closed form below v_min.**
- For u ≤ v_min, the functions g_u∘ℓ and φ_u∘ℓ/(N₀(N₀+1)), with φ_u(v) = e^{N₀v} − (N₀+1)e^{N₀u} + N₀e^{(N₀+1)u−v},
  agree on W together with their first derivatives. So (1.1) only sees exact moments:
  - ⟨Π, ab^r⟩ = Tr(AB^r), because Weyl symmetrisation is exact for a·h(b);
  - ⟨Π, 1⟩ = 2;
  - ⟨Π, e^{−ℓ}⟩ = G (note 09, Tool 0.3).
- For u ∈ [ζ₁, v_min] this gives

  N₀(N₀+1) T(u) = C(u) := Tr(AB^r) − e^{N₀ζ₂} − (N₀+1)e^{N₀u} + N₀e^{(N₀+1)u} c,  c := G − e^{−ζ₂},   (1.3)
  f(u) = 1 − c e^{u}.   (1.4)

- **The constant c.** By Ando–Hiai, λ_max(A #_{1−θ} B) ≤ e^{ζ₂}. So both eigenvalues of
  A⁻¹ #_{1−θ} B⁻¹ = (A #_{1−θ} B)⁻¹ are ≥ e^{−ζ₂}, and c > 0. Ando–Hiai also gives G ≤ Tr e^{−Z}, so c ≤ e^{−ζ₁}.
- **The minimum of C.** C′(u) = N₀(N₀+1)e^{N₀u}(ce^{u} − 1). So C has its global minimum at u_c := −log c ≥ ζ₁, where
  C(u_c) = Tr(AB^r) − e^{N₀ζ₂} − c^{−N₀}.

**Theorem 1.** Let A, B be positive definite 2 × 2 matrices and θ ∈ (0, 1/2]. Then TF holds at every level if and only
if C(min(u_c, v_min)) ≥ 0. In particular TF holds whenever C(u_c) ≥ 0, which is (C).

*Proof.*
- **Above ζ₂.** κ^θ has no mass above ζ₂, so T(u) = ∫_u^∞ e^{N₀v} m(v) dv ≥ 0 for u ≥ ζ₂.
- **Below ζ₁.** For u ≤ ζ₁ (≤ v_min), f(u) = e^{u}(Tr e^{−Z} − G) ≥ 0 by Ando–Hiai. So T is nonincreasing there.
- **On [ζ₁, ζ₂].** Here #{i : ζ_i > u} = 1.
  - On [ζ₁, v_min], e^{−u}f = e^{−u} − c is strictly decreasing, by (1.4).
  - On [v_min, ζ₂], e^{−u}f is nondecreasing, by Lemmas 1.1 and 1.2.
  - f(ζ₂) = m(ζ₂) ≥ 0.
- **Case u_c ≥ v_min.** Then f ≥ 0 on [ζ₁, v_min], hence on all of [ζ₁, ζ₂] by the monotonicity. So T is nonincreasing
  on (−∞, ζ₂] and T ≥ T(ζ₂) ≥ 0 everywhere. Also C(v_min) = N₀(N₀+1)T(v_min) ≥ 0.
- **Case u_c < v_min.**
  - f > 0 on [ζ₁, u_c) and f < 0 on (u_c, v_min].
  - On [v_min, ζ₂], e^{−u}f increases from a negative value to f(ζ₂)e^{−ζ₂} ≥ 0. So f changes sign once more, at some
    u′ ≤ ζ₂.
  - By (1.2), T decreases on (−∞, u_c], increases on [u_c, u′], and decreases on [u′, ζ₂] to T(ζ₂) ≥ 0.
  - Hence min T = min(T(u_c), a number ≥ 0), and T(u_c) = C(u_c)/(N₀(N₀+1)) by (1.3).
- In both cases TF ⟺ C(min(u_c, v_min)) ≥ 0. Since C(min(u_c, v_min)) ≥ C(u_c), (C) suffices. Finally C(u_c) ≥ 0 is
  (C) after taking the θ-th power. ∎

**Remarks.**
- **The bottom level is not enough.** The minimum of T sits at u_c > ζ₁ whenever u_c < v_min. So the bottom-level
  inequality T(ζ₁) ≥ 0 does not imply TF.
- **Equality.** For commuting pairs both factors of (C) are explicit: Tr(AB^r) − λ₂^{N₀} = λ₁^{N₀} and G − λ₂⁻¹ = λ₁⁻¹,
  with λ_i = e^{ζ_i}. So the product in (C) is 1.
- **Reading of (C).**
  - Golden–Thompson gives Tr(AB^r) ≥ λ₁^{N₀} + λ₂^{N₀}, so (Tr(AB^r) − λ₂^{N₀})^θ is an upper estimate of λ₁.
  - Ando–Hiai gives G ≤ λ₁⁻¹ + λ₂⁻¹, so 1/(G − λ₂⁻¹) is another upper estimate of λ₁.
  - (C) says the Golden–Thompson estimate is the larger of the two.
- **Hyperbolic form.** Normalise det A = det B = 1. The positive definite 2 × 2 matrices of determinant 1 form the
  hyperbolic space H³ (curvature −1, cosh d(X, Y) = Tr(X⁻¹Y)/2).
  - Put O = I, P = A⁻¹, R = B⁻¹ and Q = B^r. Then O lies on the geodesic RQ at fraction θ from R.
  - M = A⁻¹ #_{1−θ} B⁻¹ lies on the geodesic RP at fraction θ from R. So OM is a "θ-midline" of the triangle PQR.
  - E := λ_max(log Q − log P) is the length of PQ in the comparison triangle in the tangent space at O. Then
    d(P, Q) ≥ E (Golden–Thompson) and d(O, M) ≤ θE (Ando–Hiai).
  - (C) reads (2 cosh d(P,Q) − e^{E})^θ · (2 cosh d(O,M) − e^{−θE}) ≥ 1. In words: the excess of a side over its
    comparison length pays for the deficit of the θ-midline. (Checked numerically to 56 digits, `hyperbolic_form.py`.)

## 2. Proposition 2 and the normalised margin

**2.1 Hyperbolic coordinates.**
- **Normalisation.** Both factors of (C) are invariant under A → sA, B → s′B and under unitary conjugation. So assume
  det A = det B = 1: A = e^{a x̂·σ⃗} and B = e^{b ŷ·σ⃗} with a, b ≥ 0 and unit vectors with x̂·ŷ = w ∈ [−1, 1].
- **Variables.** Put c = rb and R_E = |a x̂ + c ŷ| = (a² + c² + 2acw)^{1/2}. Then N₀Z = (a x̂ + c ŷ)·σ⃗, so
  λ₂^{N₀} = e^{R_E}. The condition numbers are κ(A) = e^{2a} and κ(B^r) = e^{2c}.
- **Lemma 2.1.**
  - (i) Tr(AB^r) = 2[cosh a cosh c + w sinh a sinh c].
  - (ii) For determinant-one positive X, Y and t ∈ [0, 1], X #_t Y = [sinh((1−t)δ) X + sinh(tδ) Y]/sinh δ, where
    cosh δ = Tr(X⁻¹Y)/2. Hence G = 2[sinh(θd) cosh a + sinh((1−θ)d) cosh b]/sinh d with
    cosh d = cosh a cosh b − w sinh a sinh b.
  - *Proof of (ii).* Both sides are covariant under X → SXS*, Y → SYS* with det S = 1. This reduces to X = I,
    Y = diag(e^δ, e^{−δ}), where the identity sinh((1−t)δ) + sinh(tδ)e^{±δ} = sinh(δ)e^{±tδ} gives Y^t. ∎

**2.2 The margin.** Put ε_P := Tr(AB^r) − 2cosh R_E (the Golden–Thompson gap) and ε_G := 2cosh(θR_E) − G (the
Ando–Hiai gap). Then (C) is equivalent to

  F := θ log(1 + e^{R_E}ε_P) + log(1 − e^{−θR_E}ε_G) ≥ 0.

**Proposition 2.** If ε_P ≥ 1 − e^{−R_E}, then F ≥ 0. In matrix form: (C) holds whenever
Tr(AB^r) − Tr e^{log A + r log B} ≥ (det A)^{1/2}(det B)^{r/2}.

*Proof.* det(A #_{1−θ} B) = 1, so G ≥ 2 and 1 − e^{−θR_E}ε_G = e^{−θR_E}(G − e^{−θR_E}) ≥ e^{−θR_E}. Hence
F ≥ θ[log(1 + e^{R_E}ε_P) − R_E] ≥ 0. For the matrix form, ε_P is the Golden–Thompson gap divided by
(det A det B^r)^{1/2}, and e^{−R_E} = λ_min(e^{N₀Z})/det(e^{N₀Z})^{1/2}. ∎

**2.3 Divided differences.** Both gaps vanish at w = ±1, where the pair commutes. Newton's interpolation formula in w
gives exact factorisations. Here Ch(z) := cosh √z, U_t(cosh d) := sinh(td)/sinh d, q := (1 − w²)a²c², and square
brackets are divided differences:
- ε_P = 8q Ch[(a−c)², R_E², (a+c)²];
- ε_G = (θ/(1−θ))² q E_G with

  E_G = 2[S_a S_b V[C₋, C, C₊] − 4θ²(1−θ)² Ch[θ²(a−c)², θ²R_E², θ²(a+c)²]],

  where S_x = (sinh x/x)², V(C) = U_θ(C) cosh a + U_{1−θ}(C) cosh b, C₋ = cosh(a−b), C = cosh d and C₊ = cosh(a+b).
  Both factorisations follow from Newton's formula f(w) − (affine interpolant) = (w − 1)(w + 1) f[−1, 1, w], because
  R_E² and C are affine in w and both gaps vanish at w = ±1.

So F = θqR with R explicit. All divided differences of Ch are positive and increasing in each node (positive Taylor
coefficients). The divided differences of U_t are positive and decreasing in each node ≥ 1, by the Stieltjes
representation U_t(C) = (sin πt/π) ∫₀^∞ λ^{−t} dλ/(1 + 2Cλ + λ²). These monotonicities give box bounds for R.

**2.4 Three more ingredients** (full proofs in `PROOF.md`, Lemmas 3.5–3.7 of the code folder).
- **The commuting faces.** At w = ±1, R equals 4H, where H ≥ 0 is the function of the E-proof of note 09. So R ≥ 0 on
  both commuting faces (rigorous there).
- **The w-derivative.** ∂_wR ≥ ac·D with an explicit lower bound D. Where D > 0, R increases from its face value.
- **Thin layers.** For large a and c, the region where ε_P < 1 is an exponentially thin layer at w = ±1. Concavity of
  ε_P in w and explicit slope bounds for ε_G at the faces certify F ≥ 0 on a whole column w ∈ [−1, 1] in one step.

## 3. Theorem 3: the bounded region (computer-assisted)

**Theorem 3.** For all θ ∈ (0, 1/2], a, c ∈ [0, 40] and w ∈ [−1, 1], inequality (C) holds.

Consequently (Theorem 1), TF holds for every θ ∈ (0, 1/2] and every 2 × 2 pair with κ(A) ≤ e^{80} and
κ(B)^{(1−θ)/θ} ≤ e^{80}.

*Proof (computer-assisted).*
- **Boxes.** The region [0, 1/2] × [0, 40]² × [−1, 1] in (θ, a, c, w) is bisected adaptively. Each leaf carries one
  certificate, checked in Arb ball arithmetic (python-flint):
  - **P:** the rigorous lower bound of R on the box is > 0;
  - **I:** the lower bound of ε_P is ≥ 1 (Proposition 2);
  - **W:** the thin-layer certificate on a whole column w ∈ [−1, 1];
  - **D:** ∂_wR > 0 on a column starting at w = −1, combined with R(w = −1) ≥ 0.
- **Runs.** θ ∈ [0, 1/4]: 103,074 leaves (W 33,675, P 54,850, I 14,508, D 41). θ ∈ [1/4, 1/2]: 80,347 leaves (W 26,420,
  P 45,883, I 8,027, D 17). No failures.
- **Coverage.** Exact rational arithmetic on the leaf list: every leaf is certified, the leaves lie in the domain,
  their interiors are pairwise disjoint (5,245,022 exact pair tests), and their total volume equals the volume of the
  domain. ∎
- **Degenerate boundaries inside the region** are all covered: commuting pairs in both orientations, one matrix
  scalar, θ → 0, the identity corner at θ = 1/2 (where TF is tight to order h⁵, note 09, Section 2.3), and the
  exponentially thin near-commuting layers of ill-conditioned pairs.

**Checks.**
- **Tests in the code folder.** The ball enclosures agree with an independent 60-digit implementation of Lemma 2.1
  (40 points, including w within 10⁻⁸ of ±1). They agree with the E-proof's H on the faces (12 digits). The box bounds
  hold at 800 + 600 random interior points. The W certificate holds at 2,160 points, including points inside the thin
  layers at up to 160 digits.
- **Our re-run.** We re-ran the independent smaller certification (a, c ≤ 20) from the published scripts. It gives the
  same leaf counts (17,019 and 22,803 leaves, 0 failures) and coverage OK.
- **Our independent checks** (code written from the statements only):
  - `check_thmA.py`: for 8 random pairs, the exact Bloch-sphere value of T at u* = min(u_c, v_min) equals the closed
    form (1.3). On a fine grid of [ζ₁, ζ₂], the minimum of T is at u* or at ζ₂, where T ≥ 0 trivially, as Theorem 1
    predicts.
  - `soundness_sample.py`: 300 random points in randomly chosen certified leaves of the final runs, including points in
    the thin layers down to 1 − |w| = 10⁻³⁰. (C) is evaluated from the 2 × 2 matrices directly at 80 digits, with no
    hyperbolic closed forms. There are no violations.

## 4. Theorem 4: the far region

Notation of Section 2: det A = det B = 1, the parameters a, c, w, b = θc/(1−θ) ≤ c, and R_E, ε_P, ε_G, F. Put:
- shc(x) = sinh(x)/x, and ψ(R) = R/(1 − e^{−R}), which is increasing with ψ(R) ≥ max(1, R);
- s_θ = sin(πθ)/π ≤ θ, d = |a − b| and C₋ = cosh d;
- V(C) = U_θ(C) cosh a + U_{1−θ}(C) cosh b. Then G = 2V(C) with C = cosh a cosh b − w sinh a sinh b ≥ C₋.

**Lemma 4.1 (pointwise criterion).** If ε_P ≥ 1, or if Y_G := e^{−θR_E}ε_G ≤ ½ min(1, θψ(R_E)) ε_P, then F ≥ 0.

*Proof.* If ε_P ≥ 1 − e^{−R_E}, use Proposition 2. Otherwise X_P := e^{R_E}ε_P < e^{R_E} − 1. Concavity of
log(1 + x) on [0, e^{R_E} − 1] gives log(1 + X_P) ≥ ψ(R_E)ε_P. The hypothesis gives Y_G ≤ ½, so
−log(1 − Y_G) ≤ 2Y_G ≤ θψ(R_E)ε_P. ∎

**Lemma 4.2 (slopes and integrals).**
- (a) ε_P is concave in w and vanishes at w = ±1. Its slope ∂_wε_P = 2ac[shc(a)shc(c) − shc(R_E)] is decreasing.
  Hence ε_P(w) ≥ (1 − |w|)ε_P(0), ε_P(w) ≥ (1 − w)(−∂_wε_P(v)) for v ≤ w, and ε_P(w) ≥ (1 + w)∂_wε_P(v) for v ≥ w.
- (b) ε_G vanishes at w = ±1, and ∂_wε_G = 2θ²ac·shc(θR_E) − 2|V′(C)| sinh a sinh b. Since |V′| is decreasing in
  C and C ≥ C₋, −∂_wε_G ≤ K₊ := 2|V′(C₋)| sinh a sinh b.
- (c) The Stieltjes representation U_t(C) = (sin πt/π) ∫₀^∞ λ^{−t} dλ/(1 + 2Cλ + λ²) gives
  V(C) = s_θ ∫₀^∞ λ^{−θ}(cosh a + λ cosh b) dλ/(1 + 2Cλ + λ²). At C₋ the denominator is (λ + e^d)(λ + e^{−d}).
  Splitting the integrals at e^{±d} gives

  |V′(C₋)| ≤ s_θ[2e^{−(2−θ)d}(1.07 + min(1/θ, 2d)) cosh a + 6.8e^{−(1+θ)d} cosh b],
  V″(C₋) ≤ s_θ[e^{−(3−θ)d}(5.49 + min(8/θ, 16d)) cosh a + 22.3e^{−(2+θ)d} cosh b].

**Lemma 4.3 (two thin layers).** Let E₀ := ε_P(0) ≥ 1, w₁ = 1 − 1/E₀, w₂ = −1 + 1/E₀, R_i = R_E(w_i) and
P₊ := −∂_wε_P(w₁). Suppose

  (L+) P₊ > 0 and e^{−θR₁}K₊ ≤ ½ min(1, θψ(R₁)) P₊,  and  (L−) shc(a)shc(c) − shc(R₂) ≥ 2θ.

Then F ≥ 0 for all w.

*Proof.*
- On [w₂, w₁], ε_P ≥ 1.
- On [w₁, 1), ε_P ≥ (1 − w)P₊ and Y_G ≤ (1 − w)e^{−θR₁}K₊. Apply Lemma 4.1.
- On (−1, w₂], ε_P ≥ (1 + w)·2ac[shc(a)shc(c) − shc(R₂)], and Y_G ≤ (1 + w)·2θ²ac because e^{−x}shc(x) ≤ 1. Apply
  Lemma 4.1. ∎

**Lemma 4.4 (a condition uniform in w).** Let t = |a − c|, M_P = (t cosh t − sinh t)/t³ and
M_G = (sinh a/a)²(sinh b/b)² V″(C₋). If

  (T) e^{−θt} (θ/(1−θ))² M_G ≤ ½ min(1, θψ(t)) M_P,

then F ≥ 0 for all w.

*Proof.* The factorisations of Section 2.3 and the monotonicity of divided differences give ε_P ≥ q M_P and
ε_G ≤ (θ/(1−θ))² q M_G. Also R_E ≥ t. Apply Lemma 4.1. ∎

**Theorem 4.** For θ ∈ (0, 1/2] and max(a, c) ≥ 40, F ≥ 0 for all w ∈ [−1, 1].

*Proof.* Every estimate is written out in `PROOF.md`, Section 6. Let m = min(a, c) and M = max(a, c). If m = 0 the
pair commutes.
- **m ≥ 1: Lemma 4.3.** Put h = ac/(a + c) ≥ 40/41 and Δ = |a − b| + (c − b). Then Δ ≥ M − m.
  - E₀ ≥ (½ − e^{−h})e^{a+c} ≥ 0.123·e^{a+c}. So the layers are exponentially thin, and R₁ = a + c − δ with
    δ ≤ 3·10⁻¹⁶.
  - P₊ ≥ e^{a+c}((1 − 3·10⁻¹⁶)h − ½).
  - Lemma 4.2(c) gives e^{−θR₁}K₊ ≤ (1 + 10⁻¹⁵)·e^{a+c}·θ(4.47 + 2Δ)e^{−Δ}. The key step is an exact identity: after
    substituting c = (1−θ)b/θ, the two exponents that occur are −Δ and −Δ − 2θ(a − b) when a ≥ b, and
    −Δ − 2(1−θ)(b − a) and −Δ when a < b.
  - So (L+) reduces to (4.47 + 2Δ)e^{−Δ} ≤ const·((1 − 3·10⁻¹⁶)h − ½). This holds because h is large when m is large,
    and Δ ≥ M − m is large when m is small.
  - (L−) follows from shc(M)/shc(M − m + 1) ≥ e^{m−1}(M − m + 1)/M ≥ 1 and R₂ ≤ M − m + 1.
- **0 < m < 1: Lemma 4.4.** Then t > 39, and the right side of (T) is at least min(1, θt)(t − 1)e^t/(4t³). By
  Lemma 4.2(c) the left side is at most:
  - 776θ²/t² if a = M;
  - 258θ² if c = M and b < 1;
  - 193/t² if c = M and b ≥ 1 (then θt ≥ 0.95). ∎

**Proof of the Main Theorem.**
- If A and B commute, T ≡ 0. Otherwise Theorem 1 reduces TF to (C).
- (C) is invariant under A → sA, B → s′B and unitary conjugation. After normalising, it reads F(θ, a, c, w) ≥ 0.
- This is Theorem 3 if max(a, c) ≤ 40 and Theorem 4 if max(a, c) ≥ 40.
- For θ > 1/2 exchange A and B: TF is symmetric under (A, B, θ) ↔ (B, A, 1 − θ). ∎

**Checks of Theorem 4.**
- `tail_constants.py` verifies all 39 numerical constants of the proof in Arb ball arithmetic (200 bits) or exact
  rational arithmetic.
- `tail_check.py` evaluates every intermediate inequality of the proof at 4,090 far-region points (two seeds, 80 digits;
  θ down to 10⁻⁴, max(a, c) up to 300, min(a, c) down to 10⁻⁶). It finds no violation. The largest left/right ratios
  are 0.0064 for (L+) (at θ = ½, a = c = 40), 4.3·10⁻¹⁶ for (L−) and 4.7·10⁻¹⁶ for (T).
- `scan_far.py` evaluates F itself at 3,000 points with max(a, c) ∈ [150, 300], including layers down to
  1 ∓ w ≈ 10⁻³⁵⁹, at 520 digits. No value is negative.
- **Our re-run.** We re-ran `tail_constants.py` and seed 2 of `tail_check.py` from the published scripts; both logs
  are identical to the published ones.
- **Our independent check** (`independent-checks/tail_independent.py`, written from the statements only):
  - it uses exact closed forms for V′ and V″ at 150 digits;
  - it tests (L+), (L−), (T), the slope facts and F itself at 320 random far-region points;
  - it finds no violation. The largest (L+) ratio is 7.8·10⁻⁶ and the largest (T) ratio is 4.9·10⁻¹⁹.

## 5. Corollary 5: two-point spectra in every dimension

**Corollary 5.** Let A and B be positive definite d × d matrices that each have at most two distinct eigenvalues.
Then TF holds for (A, B) at every θ ∈ (0, 1).

*Proof.*
- **Two projections.** Write A = α₁P + α₂(1 − P) and B = β₁Q + β₂(1 − Q) with orthogonal projections P and Q. By
  the two-subspace theorem (Jordan; Halmos 1969), C^d is an orthogonal sum of subspaces of dimension 1 and 2 that
  reduce both P and Q. So (A, B) is a direct sum of 1 × 1 pairs and 2 × 2 pairs.
- **Additivity.** Both sides of TF are additive over direct sums: Tr e^{i(xA+yB)} is additive, so Π is, and
  Tr g_u(Z) is additive.
- **Blocks.** On 1 × 1 blocks T ≡ 0. On 2 × 2 blocks TF holds by the Main Theorem. ∎

The lower half itself was already known in this case (note 03: A or B with two distinct eigenvalues).

## 6. What does not extend to d ≥ 3

- **The lens lemma is false for d ≥ 3** (`general-d/lens_general.py` in the code folder). For d = 3 and 4, Λ(u) = Π^θ((u, ∞)) exceeds
  #{i : ζ_i > u} by up to 0.93, just above middle eigenvalues of Z. So the monotonicity used in Theorem 1 fails
  between consecutive eigenvalues.
- **Where the excess comes from.** Split Π along the eigenvalue branches λ₁(φ) < … < λ_d(φ) of cos φ A + sin φ B.
  - The outer pair (λ₁, λ_d) is the support function of the joint numerical range W. Its inverse Radon transform Π_W
    has interior density −(1/4π²) ∫ dφ / (h_W(φ) − y·e_φ)² < 0 for every convex W, plus a positive boundary layer.
    Caps still have Π_W-mass 1 and lenses have nonpositive mass, so the argument of Lemma 1.2 gives
    ⟨Π_W, 1[ℓ > u]⟩ ≤ 1 on (v_min, v_max) (a sketch; we have not written out the boundary analysis for a general W).
  - The inner branches sweep the interior of W and carry positive singular parts along the inner Kippenhahn curves.
    That is where the count overshoots.
- **What does extend: the bottom window.** In every dimension Π is supported in W (its projections are the spectral
  measures of cos φ A + sin φ B). So for u < v_min the closed-form argument of Section 1.4 applies verbatim. On
  [ζ_k, ζ_{k+1}] ∩ (−∞, v_min],

  N₀(N₀+1) T(u) = Tr(AB^r) − Σ_{i>k} e^{N₀ζ_i} − k(N₀+1)e^{N₀u} + N₀e^{(N₀+1)u}(G − Σ_{i>k} e^{−ζ_i}),

  with f(u) = k − e^{u}(G − Σ_{i>k} e^{−ζ_i}). So TF below v_min is a finite list of closed-form trace inequalities in
  every dimension. For d = 2 only k = 1 occurs, by the ordering of Section 0.
- **Consequence.** The open part of TF for d ≥ 3 is the range (v_min, ζ_d). A proof there cannot be a level-by-level
  count. It has to use the exponential weight of the tail. TF itself survives every search (note 08, Section 4).

## 7. Related work

- **Heinävaara's measure.** O. Heinävaara, *Tracial joint spectral measures*, arXiv:2310.03227, Invent. Math. 239
  (2025) 505–526. Its positivity is used in Theorem 1 (f(ζ₂) = m(ζ₂) ≥ 0) and in the TF framework of note 08.
- **The case d = 2 of the lower half.** S. Furuichi, K. Kuriyama and K. Yanagi, Linear Algebra Appl. 430 (2009)
  2271–2276, Theorem 2.2.
- **Ando–Hiai log-majorisation.** T. Ando and F. Hiai, Linear Algebra Appl. 197/198 (1994) 113–131.
- **Two subspaces.** P. R. Halmos, *Two subspaces*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 144 (1969) 381–389.
- **Orders for joint spectral measures.** L. Schmitt, *Relative entropy representations via tracial joint spectral
  measures*, arXiv:2609.12547, studies convex-order statements for likelihood-ratio laws built from Heinävaara's
  measure. TF is a statement of the same kind, an order between a projected joint spectral measure and an eigenvalue
  law. It is a tilted first-order dominance on log-linear rays, and the plain convex order is false in our setting
  (note 08, Section 5).
- **Ball arithmetic.** F. Johansson, *Arb: efficient arbitrary-precision midpoint-radius interval arithmetic*, IEEE
  Trans. Comput. 66 (2017) 1281–1292 (used through python-flint).
